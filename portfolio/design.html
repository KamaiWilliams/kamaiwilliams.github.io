Selling Mexican

2026

Made using ReadyMag & Figma

Made in collaboration with Regina Cervantes, Jess Kuronen & Alexander Tochilovsky

Starting in June 2025, Regina Cervantes and I worked together to bring Regina’s idea for an archive of Mexican restaurant ephemera to life. Driven by her interest in how Mexican identity is represented through restaurant design, the project grew into a collection of materials gathered across five New York City neighborhoods. Across more than 75 restaurants, we collected 57 menus, 18 business cards, 5 stickers, 3 to-go packages, 1 receipt, 192 photographs of signage, 1 evangelization card, and 1 Know Your Rights card. We also conducted 3 interviews with restaurant owners. Through the collection and organization of these materials we created an archive that documents the visual strategies used to sell “Mexico” in New York City.

A selection of menus from the archive.

We built an index of “Mexicanness” by extracting visual symbols, identifying similarities, and organizing recurring elements into categories. This process helped us compare the colors, typefaces, imagery, and decorative motifs used across restaurant materials.

1 / 7

Restaurants we visited, photographed during fieldwork.

We then conducted a survey with 76 participants from a range of age groups and backgrounds to assess their perceptions of these visual categories. Participants were asked to associate menu colors, typefaces, and symbols with one of ten randomly assigned cuisines. I developed the survey with the help of Claude Code and connected its JavaScript to a Google Sheet, where responses were recorded for analysis. This gave us a way to investigate patterns in how people interpreted visual cues and associated them with different cuisines.

1 / 36

Survey prototype, from participant introduction to visual association questions.

We combined our archive, survey responses, and historical research into a digital publication. Bringing these sources together allowed us to connect our collected materials with the patterns and questions that emerged throughout the project.

As the culmination of our year-long project, we translated our findings into a long-form digital publication featuring original essays, data visualizations, infographics, and graphic analysis, now archived in the Lubalin Center's Digital FlatFile ↗.

Typefaces

2026

An exploration of type and iconography through a daily emoji-making practice using glyphs from a single typeface. After 30 days, I compiled the emojis into a catalog that showcases how they can function within visual culture by inserting them into found Getty Images and everyday scenarios.

House of Yes Club Rebrand

2025

A collaborative rebrand of Brooklyn’s iconic nightlife venue rooted in the club’s DIY origins and expressive spirit. The identity system embraces fluidity and experimentation through a modular visual language inspired by the ever-changing energy of the space.

Redesigning Bus Stops

2024

A speculative redesign of NYC bus stops that replaces traditional hard benches with playground-inspired swing seating to create a more playful and engaging public waiting space. The project explores how everyday infrastructure can introduce moments of stimulation and joy into daily routines while also serving as an introduction to Rhino 8 and 3D printing.
